📚 Comprehensive Investment & Compounding Guide

Master the fundamentals of long-term wealth creation, compounding math, and portfolio efficiency.

🌱 What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest earned on both your initial principal investment AND the accumulated interest from prior periods. Unlike simple interest—which only pays interest on the original starting deposit—compound interest creates an exponential mathematical snowball effect over time.

⏳ Why Time in the Market Matters Most

In compound interest mathematics, the exponent is time ($t$). Starting just 5 or 10 years earlier can double or triple your final portfolio balance even if you contribute less total money out of pocket.

🔄 Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) vs. Lump-Sum

Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) involves investing fixed dollar amounts at steady intervals (e.g., $500 every month), automatically purchasing more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. Lump-Sum Investing deploys all available capital immediately. Both are proven strategies; DCA reduces psychological volatility risk.

🛒 How Inflation Affects Real Purchasing Power

Inflation steadily erodes purchasing power over time. A nominal $1,000,000 balance in 30 years at a 2.5% inflation rate will have the equivalent purchasing power of roughly $476,743 today. Always review both nominal and inflation-adjusted figures.

📉 The Hidden Drag of Investment Fees (AUM & Expense Ratios)

An ongoing 1.0% annual management fee may seem minor, but over a 30-year horizon, it can consume more than 20% to 30% of your total potential terminal portfolio value due to lost compounding opportunities.

🧮 How This Calculator Works

Our calculation engine applies standard discrete periodic compounding formulas: $$FV = P(1 + r/n)^{nt} + PMT \times \frac{(1 + r/n)^{nt} - 1}{r/n}$$ accounting for annual contribution increases, discrete monthly step-ups, fee drags, and compounding frequency.
