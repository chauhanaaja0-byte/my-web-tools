Why Roth IRA Growth Can Be Valuable

A Roth IRA is one of the most powerful wealth-building tools available to individual US investors. Because contributions are made with after-tax dollars, qualified distributions in retirement—including decades of compound investment growth—can be 100% tax-free under current IRS rules.

The 5-Year Rule: To qualify for tax-free and penalty-free withdrawals of earnings, the account holder must generally be at least age 59½ and at least 5 tax years must have passed since the first contribution to any Roth IRA.

Roth IRA vs. 401(k)

While a workplace 401(k) often features an employer matching contribution and higher annual contribution limits, a Roth IRA offers complete investment freedom across thousands of individual stocks, index funds, and ETFs without plan administration fees. Financial planners often recommend securing any full employer 401(k) match first, then maximizing an annual Roth IRA contribution.
