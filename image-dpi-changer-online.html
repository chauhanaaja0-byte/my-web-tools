Critical Distinction: DPI vs Pixels

Understanding digital data vs print density

DPI (Dots Per Inch) describes the density of ink droplets packed into one physical inch when printed on paper. Pixel dimensions (e.g., 3000 × 2000 px) describe the actual amount of raw digital image data stored in the file.

Changing DPI metadata alone does NOT automatically add pixels, increase sharpness, or enhance image quality. For example, a 3000 × 2000 pixel image remains 3000 × 2000 pixels whether its metadata tag says 72 DPI or 300 DPI. What changes is the intended physical print size: at 300 DPI it prints at 10 × 6.67 inches, whereas at 72 DPI it prints at 41.67 × 27.78 inches.

Frequently Asked Questions

DPI & Print Resolution FAQ

What is DPI?

DPI stands for Dots Per Inch. It is a measurement of physical print density that indicates how many individual dots of ink are laid down per linear inch of printed material. In digital image files, DPI is an embedded metadata tag that tells printing drivers and layout applications how large to render an image on paper.

How do I change an image to 300 DPI?

Upload your image, click the '300 DPI' preset button, choose 'Change DPI Metadata' (for lossless processing without altering pixels), and click 'Apply 300 DPI & Prepare Image'. Then download your updated file.

Does changing DPI increase image quality?

No. Changing the DPI tag does not add new detail or increase camera sensor resolution. An image that has 1000 × 1000 pixels still has 1000 × 1000 pixels regardless of whether its tag says 72 DPI or 300 DPI.

Are my images uploaded to a remote server?

No. All processing runs entirely inside your browser client using HTML5 Canvas and typed array memory buffers. No files or photos ever leave your device.
