Clinical Guide: Home Blood Pressure Monitoring, Ranges & Hemodynamics

Home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) provides critical insight into your cardiovascular health, eliminating the "white coat effect" often observed in clinical offices. Blood pressure naturally oscillates throughout the day in response to physical activity, mental stress, circadian hormone surges, and metabolic demand.

Understanding Systolic vs. Diastolic

Systolic pressure (top number) represents the peak pressure in arterial walls when the heart contracts. Diastolic pressure (bottom number) measures arterial resistance between beats when the ventricles refill with blood.

Pulse Pressure & Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)

Pulse Pressure (Systolic − Diastolic) reflects arterial compliance; values >60 mmHg can indicate arterial stiffness. MAP estimates the constant perfusion pressure reaching vital capillary beds (70–100 mmHg).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is considered a normal blood pressure reading? ▼

According to the American Heart Association (AHA), a normal blood pressure reading for an adult is a systolic pressure under 120 mm Hg AND a diastolic pressure under 80 mm Hg (<120/<80 mmHg). In the UK (NHS/NICE), ideal blood pressure is generally considered between 90/60 mmHg and 120/80 mmHg.

How do I calculate my 7-day blood pressure average? ▼

To calculate your 7-day blood pressure average, record your blood pressure twice daily (once in the morning and once in the evening) for 7 consecutive days. Sum all your systolic readings and divide by the total number of readings to get your average systolic; repeat the same process for your diastolic readings.

What is the difference between USA (AHA) and UK (NHS/NICE) blood pressure guidelines? ▼

The 2017 AHA/ACC guidelines in the United States classify Stage 1 Hypertension starting at 130/80 mm Hg. In contrast, the UK NICE guidelines (NG136) use a home monitoring threshold of 135/85 mm Hg and a clinical/office threshold of 140/90 mm Hg before diagnosing hypertension.

What is pulse pressure and why does it matter? ▼

Pulse pressure is the numerical difference between your systolic and diastolic blood pressure readings (Systolic − Diastolic). A standard resting pulse pressure is between 40 and 60 mm Hg. Consistently high pulse pressure (>60 mm Hg) can indicate arterial stiffness.
