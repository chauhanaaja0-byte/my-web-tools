What Does PITI Mean in Mortgage Calculations?

In American mortgage lending, PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance. These four elements form the baseline of your monthly housing expense:

  • Principal: The portion of each monthly payment that pays down your original loan balance and builds your home equity.
  • Interest: The cost charged by the mortgage lender for borrowing the principal funds.
  • Taxes: Annual municipal and county property taxes, divided into 12 monthly installments deposited into your escrow reserve.
  • Insurance: Annual homeowners hazard insurance, plus Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) if your down payment is below 20%.

How Does Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) Work?

When purchasing a residential home with a conventional loan and a down payment of less than 20% (an LTV above 80%), mortgage lenders require Private Mortgage Insurance to protect against default. Under federal law (the Homeowners Protection Act of 1998), homeowners can request PMI cancellation when their principal balance reaches 80% of original value, and lenders must cancel it at 78% scheduled amortization.

What does PITI mean? +

What is Loan-to-Value (LTV)? +
LTV is the ratio of your mortgage balance compared to the property purchase price. An LTV over 80% typically triggers PMI.
How is the monthly mortgage payment calculated? +
Using the standard formula: M = P * [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1], where P is principal, r is monthly rate, and n is total payments.
