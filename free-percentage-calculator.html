Percentage Formula Reference

Standard mathematical formulas used across textbook algebra, statistics, and commerce.

1. Percentage of a Number

Result = (P ÷ 100) × V

Where P is the percentage and V is the total value. Example: 20% of 250 = (20/100) × 250 = 50.

2. Percent of Total (What Percent Is X of Y?)

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Where Part is the numerator and Whole is the denominator. Example: 50 is (50/250) × 100 = 20% of 250.

3. Percentage Increase

New Value = Original + [Original × (Increase % ÷ 100)]

Alternatively: Original × (1 + Increase % / 100). Example: $80 + 25% = $80 + $20 = $100.

4. Percentage Decrease

New Value = Original - [Original × (Decrease % ÷ 100)]

Alternatively: Original × (1 - Decrease % / 100). Example: $120 - 15% = $120 - $18 = $102.

5. Percentage Change

Change % = [(New Value - Old Value) ÷ |Old Value|] × 100

Measures proportional shift relative to the initial baseline. Negative result indicates a decrease.

6. Percentage Difference

Difference % = [|A - B| ÷ ((|A| + |B|) ÷ 2)] × 100

Divides the absolute variance by the mean of both quantities when neither value is an older baseline.

Understanding Percentages: Core Concepts

A comprehensive educational guide to percentages in modern mathematics and commerce.

What Is a Percentage Calculator?

A percentage calculator is a digital computational tool designed to solve mathematical problems involving fractions of 100. The term percent originates from the Latin phrase per centum, which literally translates to "by the hundred." In modern society, percentages are the universal language for expressing rates, discounts, taxes, interest charges, statistical changes, and financial performances.

Rather than manually juggling long decimal divisions or converting fractions with uncommon denominators, an online percentage calculator instantly standardizes values onto a 1-to-100 scale, making comparisons intuitive and immediate.

How to Calculate a Percentage of a Number

Finding a percentage of a given number is one of the most frequent arithmetic tasks in daily life. The standard mathematical procedure consists of two simple steps:

  • Step 1: Convert the percentage into a decimal by dividing the percentage by 100 (move the decimal point two positions to the left).
  • Step 2: Multiply that resulting decimal by the total number.

For instance, to calculate 15% of $80: divide 15 by 100 to obtain 0.15. Then multiply 0.15 by 80, which equals $12.00. This same formula applies whether you are computing sales tax, retirement contribution withholdings, or grocery store discounts.

How to Find What Percent One Number Is of Another

When you have a part and a whole and wish to discover the proportional share as a percentage, you invert the process:

Formula: (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For example, if an energy-efficient light bulb uses 12 watts compared to a traditional 60-watt incandescent bulb, what percent of the original power does it draw? Dividing 12 by 60 gives 0.20. Multiplying 0.20 by 100 produces 20%. The efficient bulb uses only 20% of the energy consumed by the old model.

How to Calculate Percentage Increase

Percentage increase quantifies how much a value has grown relative to its initial starting point. Whether measuring rent hikes, population growth, or wholesale commodity price increases, the standard formula is:

New Value = Original Value + [Original Value × (Percentage ÷ 100)]

Alternatively, you can multiply the original value by (1 + Rate). If your monthly health insurance premium is $400 and goes up by 8%, multiply $400 by 1.08 to find your new monthly cost: $432.00.

How to Calculate Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease calculates the reduction of an amount from its original base. It is the cornerstone of retail sales discounts, vehicle depreciation models, and body weight tracking:

New Value = Original Value - [Original Value × (Percentage ÷ 100)]

To determine a 25% discount on an item marked at $60, compute 25% of $60 (which is $15) and subtract that from $60. The final purchase price is $45.00.

How to Calculate Percentage Change

Percentage change evaluates the degree of change between an older baseline and a newer observation. Unlike simple percentage of a number, percentage change can be positive (growth) or negative (shrinkage):

Percentage Change = [(New Value - Old Value) ÷ |Old Value|] × 100

Notice that the absolute value of the original quantity is used in the denominator to preserve proper directionality when dealing with financial losses or negative balances. If a website had 2,000 visitors last week and 2,600 visitors this week: [(2,600 - 2,000) / 2,000] × 100 = (600 / 2,000) × 100 = +30%.

Advanced Applications & Financial Analysis

Practical nuances of difference metrics, reverse formulas, business economics, and common errors.

Percentage Change vs. Percentage Difference

One of the most frequent points of confusion in data analysis is confusing percentage change with percentage difference:

  • Percentage Change: Used when there is a distinct chronological sequence or cause-and-effect relationship (e.g., stock price in January vs. December). The earlier value serves as the natural benchmark.
  • Percentage Difference: Used when comparing two coexisting, independent items where neither serves as the benchmark (e.g., comparing the price of milk in New York versus Chicago). Because neither city came first, the average of both prices serves as the denominator.

How to Calculate Percentage Difference

The percentage difference formula computes the absolute variance divided by the average of the two numbers:

Difference % = [|A - B| ÷ ((|A| + |B|) ÷ 2)] × 100

If Company A produces 80 units and Company B produces 100 units, the difference between them is: |80 - 100| / ((80 + 100) / 2) = 20 / 90 ≈ 22.22%. Regardless of whether you evaluate A compared to B or B compared to A, the percentage difference remains identical.

How Reverse Percentages Work

Reverse percentage calculations allow you to work backwards from a final, modified number to discover the original starting balance. This is indispensable when checking store receipts or reconciling tax-inclusive invoices.

A classic mistake is believing that if a price was reduced by 20%, adding 20% to the sale price will restore the original cost. That is mathematically incorrect because the discount was taken on the larger original base, whereas adding 20% to the discounted price uses the smaller reduced base.

To find the original price after a 20% discount on an item marked at $80: Original = $80 ÷ (1 - 0.20) = $80 ÷ 0.80 = $100.00.

Using Percentages for Discounts, Price Changes & Salary Raises

Percentages simplify financial decisions:

  • Stacking Discounts: When a department store offers "20% off already reduced clearance items (marked 30% off)", the total discount is not 50%. A $100 item first becomes $70 (30% off). Then 20% of $70 ($14) is subtracted, yielding $56 (an effective 44% total discount).
  • Cost of Living & Merit Raises: Over a multi-year career, compounding salary raises significantly elevate total lifetime earnings. A 4% annual raise on a $70,000 salary yields $72,800 in year one, and $75,712 in year two due to compounding on the new baseline.

Common Percentage Calculation Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing Percentage Points with Percent: If an interest rate climbs from 3% to 4%, it rose by 1 percentage point, but the proportional increase in interest cost is 33.33%.
  • Dividing by Zero: Calculating percentage change from a starting value of zero is mathematically undefined. An increase from $0 to $50 cannot be expressed as a finite percentage.
  • Forgetting to Re-anchor Baselines: When calculating sequential increases and decreases, always calculate each step on the updated intermediate balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions regarding percentage math, calculator mechanics, and browser privacy.

How do I calculate a percentage?

To find a percentage of any number, divide the percentage by 100 to convert it into a decimal, then multiply by the total number. For example, 20% of 250 is (20 ÷ 100) × 250 = 50.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

To calculate percentage increase, multiply the original value by the percentage divided by 100 to find the addition, then add that amount to the original value.

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

Multiply the original number by the decrease percentage divided by 100, then subtract that result from the original value to arrive at the discounted total.

What is percentage change?

Percentage change expresses the relative difference between an older baseline and a newer value. It shows whether a quantity increased or decreased over time relative to where it started.

What is percentage difference?

Percentage difference measures the relative variance between two numbers without establishing one as an older baseline. It divides the absolute difference by the average of both numbers.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?

Percentage change has a chronological starting point and divides by the original value. Percentage difference compares two unordered values and divides by the average of both values.

How do I calculate reverse percentage?

To find the original amount before a discount or increase, divide the final value by 1 minus the discount rate (for decreases) or 1 plus the increase rate (for increases).

Can percentages be over 100%?

Yes. Percentages greater than 100% simply indicate that the quantity is larger than the original reference point. For example, 250% of 100 is 250.

Can percentage change be negative?
